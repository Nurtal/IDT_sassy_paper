\begin{abstract}
Computational immunology has produced high-quality mechanistic models of
individual immune compartments, yet composing ODE and agent-based models
(ABMs) across formalism boundaries still requires bespoke re-engineering.
Existing standards (SBML, CellML) address intra-formalism portability, and
prior formalism-agnostic composition frameworks provide no standardised
runtime uncertainty quantification, model-derived transfer lags, or
biological plausibility constraint engine. We propose OISA, the Orchestrated
Immune Simulation Architecture, comprising (i) the ISSL, a
formalism-agnostic JSON-LD checkpoint format with embedded runtime UQ
(uncertainty bounds propagated at every tick via the \texttt{ci\_95} schema
field); (ii) a declarative configuration graph supporting model-derived
transfer lags on edges; and (iii) an orchestrator engine with global-clock
synchronisation, causal DAG resolution, and biological plausibility
enforcement.

We validate OISA by coupling two independently published influenza
models, Miao \emph{et al.} 2010 ODE (SBML BIOMD0000000546,
unmodified) and the full spatial Sego \emph{et al.} 2020 ABM
(CompuCell3D 4.8.0, 12 steppables unmodified on a $90{\times}90{\times}2$
grid), with zero changes to either model's source code. Adapter
layers total \ttilde{}300 lines. The ABM runs as a rolling ensemble of
$N\!=\!50$ parallel CC3D instances (concurrent subprocesses, distinct
MersenneTwister seeds); runtime uncertainty bounds, the 2.5th--97.5th
percentile across the $N\!=\!50$ replicates (\S\ref{sec:valid-setup}),
originate in the stochastic ABM and propagate through the ODE at each
checkpoint. Over a 14-day simulation (56 checkpoints), the coupled system
reproduces viral kinetics consistent with Miao 2010 (peak
$V\!=\!9.0{\times}10^{6}$\,copies/mL at day~2.25, clearance to
0.05\% of peak by day~13.75) and spatial immune recruitment (median
$n_{\text{immune}}\!=\!6$ [IQR: 5--8] at day~1, growing to 57
[IQR: 55--60] at day~13), with immune temporal lag and
CTL-mediated clearance acceleration emerging from the bidirectional
coupling. To test that this composition is structural rather than an
artefact of the ODE--ABM pair, we further couple a third, categorically
distinct formalism, a continuous-time stochastic Boolean signalling
network (MaBoSS \texttt{TregModel}, 35 nodes), through the same contract;
each formalism reports its native uncertainty (ensemble percentiles for the
ODE/ABM, a Wilson interval for the Boolean model) and these compose,
verifiably, into the coupled output, giving what is to our knowledge the
first heterogeneous cross-formalism runtime UQ in an immune multi-model
simulation. The validation targets orchestration correctness (signal
routing, causal ordering, UQ propagation); as a secondary check, emergent
temporal features (immune-recruitment onset, viral-clearance day) show
face-validity against independent published murine influenza kinetics, not
biological prediction, as no coupled-configuration parameter was fit to
simultaneous in-vivo measurements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%%==============================================================
Immune-mediated diseases are inherently multi-compartmental. Influenza
unfolds across scales: viral replication interacts with innate cytokine
signalling and adaptive CTL recruitment, each on a different timescale and
best served by a different formalism, ODEs for systemic viral kinetics,
ABMs where stochastic single-cell fate decisions dominate tissue-level
recruitment. The literature has consequently produced high-quality
compartment-specific models that cannot communicate with one another.

Three bodies of prior work address adjacent problems. The COMBINE
standards ecosystem (SBML~\cite{hucka2015sbml},
CellML~\cite{clerx2020cellml}, SED-ML~\cite{waltemath2011sedml})
provides portable model representations but addresses intra-formalism
composition only: models must share one declarative format, ruling out
ABMs written in Python or Julia. Vivarium~\cite{agmon2022vivarium}
introduced formalism-agnostic composition
(\S\ref{sec:related-vivarium}) but provides no standardised runtime UQ
propagation, model-derived transfer lags, or plausibility constraint
engine. The CURE guidelines~\cite{sauro2025cure} (Credibility,
Understandability, Reproducibility, Extensibility) define what a credible
model should exhibit but prescribe no runtime infrastructure for
heterogeneous composition. The cross-formalism, multi-timescale coordination
problem remains open.

We propose OISA, the Orchestrated Immune Simulation Architecture,
to fill this gap, in three components: the ISSL formalism-agnostic state
interface (\S\ref{sec:issl}), a declarative configuration graph
(\S\ref{sec:configgraph}), and an orchestrator engine
(\S\ref{sec:orchestrator}). We demonstrate it by coupling two
independently published influenza models, using the full spatial Sego
2020 CompuCell3D ABM without modifying its 12 biological steppables, and
evaluate causal ordering, signal-flow correctness, and biological
plausibility in \S\ref{sec:validation}.

\medskip\noindent The contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The ISSL}, a formalism-agnostic checkpoint format with
        embedded provenance and runtime UQ (ensemble bounds propagated
        each tick from stochastic ABM through deterministic ODE via the
        \texttt{ci\_95} field);
  \item \textbf{A declarative configuration graph} supporting
        model-derived transfer lags on edges (delay computed dynamically
        by a lightweight transfer model, not a fixed constant);
  \item \textbf{A runtime orchestrator} with global-clock
        synchronisation, causal DAG resolution, and biological
        plausibility enforcement;
  \item \textbf{Empirical demonstration} that two independently published
        models (Miao 2010 SBML ODE + Sego 2020 CC3D spatial ABM) compose
        with zero source modifications using \ttilde{}300 lines of adapter
        code, UQ propagated end-to-end;
  \item \textbf{Cross-formalism generalisation}: a third, categorically
        distinct formalism (a stochastic Boolean MaBoSS network) coupled
        through the same contract, ensemble percentiles for the
        ODE/ABM, a native Wilson interval for the Boolean model, to
        our knowledge the first such demonstration in immune multi-model
        simulation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
%%==============================================================

\subsection{Multi-Formalism Immune Simulation Frameworks}
\label{sec:related-vivarium}

Several frameworks address multi-scale composition in computational
biology. Vivarium~\cite{agmon2022vivarium} introduced a port-based,
formalism-agnostic composition interface with hierarchical stores,
runtime topology changes, and a mature Python ecosystem. OISA shares
its formalism-agnostic principle but adds three capabilities absent
from Vivarium: (i) a standardised inter-model signal format (ISSL)
embedding runtime UQ (ensemble bounds propagated per checkpoint from
the ABM through the ODE via the \texttt{ci\_95} field), (ii)
model-derived transfer lags on edges, and (iii) a biological
plausibility constraint engine at the composition level (mass
conservation, parameter bounds, divergence monitoring); Vivarium's
store-based sharing and dynamic topology are complementary capabilities
OISA does not replicate.

PhysiCell~\cite{ghaffarizadeh2018physicell} and
PhysiBoSS~\cite{ponce2023physiboss} are the closest prior art: PhysiBoSS
embeds MaBoSS Boolean networks~\cite{stoll2017maboss} inside PhysiCell
agents. The coupling is \emph{intra-engine}, the Boolean layer is compiled
into the ABM and shares its address space, timestep, and build system, so
the formalisms cannot be developed, versioned, or uncertainty-quantified
independently, and it exposes a single homogeneous stochastic-ABM
uncertainty. OISA instead couples an \emph{externally published} MaBoSS
model (\texttt{TregModel}, from the pyMaBoSS reference
suite~\cite{pymaboss}) to the Miao2010 ODE and Sego2020 ABM through the
ISSL contract alone, each running unmodified in its own engine, and
\emph{propagates each formalism's native uncertainty across the coupling
boundary} (\S\ref{sec:xf-uq}). The community SARS-CoV-2 tissue
simulator~\cite{getz2020sarscov2}, from which Sego \emph{et al.}
2020~\cite{sego2020modular} derives, showed tissue-scale ABMs can be shared
rapidly but required a uniform CompuCell3D substrate, coupling to external
ODEs needing bespoke adapter code outside the framework. Miao \emph{et al.}
2010~\cite{miao2010influenza} provided a calibrated ODE model of murine
influenza kinetics in BioModels (BIOMD0000000546) with no standard
mechanism to couple it to tissue ABMs without rewriting either model. OISA
achieves such coupling by adding only a thin Emit/Accept layer to each, the
full CC3D spatial ABM running unmodified as an independent process.

\subsection{Simulation Interoperability Standards}

The COMBINE standards ecosystem~\cite{bergmann2014combine} has advanced
individual-model portability: SBML~\cite{hucka2015sbml} has over 1{,}000
curated BioModels entries, CellML~\cite{clerx2020cellml} analogous cardiac
and physiological coverage, and SED-ML~\cite{waltemath2011sedml} a standard
for encoding simulation experiments (Table~\ref{tab:standards}). The
distinction is architectural: SBML, CellML, and NeuroML address
\emph{intra-formalism} interoperability, exchange between tools sharing one
format, while OISA addresses \emph{inter-formalism} composition of models
with different formalisms, timescales, and output semantics. SBML's
composition package (comp) extends composition within SBML but cannot
incorporate an ABM without rewriting it, impractical and biologically
inappropriate for stochastic cellular processes.

BioSimulators~\cite{shaikh2022biosimulators} and the SED-ML/COMBINE stack
standardise how an experiment is described and re-executed across engines,
but target reproducible single-model execution, not a runtime inter-model
signal carrying an uncertainty interval. The gap OISA fills is thus neither
portability (SBML/CellML), reproducibility (SED-ML/BioSimulators), nor
port-based composition (Vivarium), but \textbf{heterogeneous
cross-formalism uncertainty propagation}: one transport contract under
which each formalism reports its \emph{native} uncertainty and the
orchestrator composes those intervals at coupling boundaries without
homogenising them (\S\ref{sec:xf-uq}, Table~\ref{tab:uq-taxonomy}).

\subsection{Immune Digital Twins}

The immune digital twin (IDT) was formalised by Laubenbacher \emph{et
al.}~\cite{laubenbacher2022roadmap} as a continuously-recalibrated
patient-specific model of the immune system. Subsequent
reviews~\cite{laubenbacher2024forum,niarakis2024idt} identify
interoperability as the primary technical barrier to clinical deployment,
a conclusion echoed by the National Academies~\cite{nasem2023digitaltwins},
model-integration consortia~\cite{karr2022modelintegration}, and
multi-scale digital-twin frameworks in adjacent
domains~\cite{viceconti2024vht}. OISA is the first runtime orchestration
architecture designed specifically for heterogeneous immune-model
composition, providing the coordination infrastructure these reviews
identify as lacking.

\section{Problem Formalisation}
%%==============================================================

\subsection{Formal Model Definition}
We define a composable simulation model $M$ as a triple
$M\!=\!(S,\mathrm{Emit},\mathrm{Accept})$, where $S$ is the model's
internal state space,
$\mathrm{Emit}\!:\!S\!\times\!T\!\to\!\mathrm{ISSL}$ maps the current
state and simulation time to a structured ISSL record, and
$\mathrm{Accept}\!:\!\mathrm{ISSL}\!\to\!S$ updates the model state in
response to an incoming inter-model signal. Any model, ODE, ABM, hybrid,
or neural surrogate, implementing Emit and Accept is OISA-compliant with no
other modification. Formalism-agnosticism follows: since the ISSL is the
only communication channel, models need only the schema of the signal they
receive, not each other's internal representation. Each biological process
is thus modelled in its natural formalism, solving composition at the
interface rather than forcing inappropriate representations on individual
compartments.

\subsection{The Composition Problem}\label{sec:composition}
A multi-model composition is a directed graph $G\!=\!(V,E)$ where each
vertex $v$ is a model $M_v$ and each edge $(u,v)$ routes a subset of $u$'s
\texttt{export\_signals} to $v$'s Accept function, with optional
transfer-model lags. The composition problem reduces to three coordination
challenges:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Heterogeneous time steps.} A global simulation clock
      (GSimT), set to the GCD of all model $\Delta t_i$, coordinates
      execution so no model receives a signal from a future tick (here the
      ODE steps at 6\,h, the ABM at 24\,h, so GSimT $=$ 6\,h).
\item \textbf{Stochastic--deterministic reconciliation.} Signals from a
      stochastic ABM to a deterministic ODE must carry uncertainty: the
      orchestrator normalises every signal to (median, bounds, unit) in the
      \texttt{ci\_95} field, ensemble percentiles for stochastic models
      ($N\!=\!50$ here), propagated bounds for deterministic ones.
\item \textbf{Causal ordering with feedback.} Immune signals drive viral
      clearance while viral load drives recruitment, a feedback loop
      resolved by a one-tick delay on DAG back-edges, preventing circular
      dependency while preserving causality at the GSimT timescale.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The OISA Architecture}
%%==============================================================

\subsection{The Internal Simulation State Log (ISSL)}\label{sec:issl}
The ISSL is a JSON-LD document emitted at each checkpoint in six
sections: (i)~\texttt{envelope} (model ID/version, GSimT timestamp,
schema URI, formalism tag, validated before any payload is read);
(ii)~\texttt{continuous\_state} (entity populations with scaled
\texttt{count}, \texttt{unit}, ensemble \texttt{ci\_95}, and model-local
\texttt{label}); (iii)~\texttt{discrete\_events} (punctual events from a
controlled \texttt{event\_type} vocabulary, with \texttt{n\_realisations}
for ABM events); (iv)~\texttt{export\_signals} (routable inter-model
signals: \texttt{signal\_id} key, \texttt{value} in declared \texttt{unit},
always biologically scaled); (v)~\texttt{internal\_parameters} (kinetic
values with \texttt{provenance} to the calibration source); and
(vi)~\texttt{watchdog} (\texttt{divergence\_score}; $>\!0.15 \Rightarrow$
\texttt{PAUSE}). JSON-LD gives both human readability and machine-parsable
semantics, letting the orchestrator parse records without knowing any
model's internal variable names.

\textit{Box 1, ISSL excerpt, ensemble-aggregated Miao 2010 ODE
checkpoint at day~1 (\texttt{count} $=$ median, \texttt{ci\_95} $=$
2.5th--97.5th percentile over $N\!=\!50$ replicates):}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=json]
{"envelope":{"model_id":"miao2010_ode","formalism":"ODE",
  "model_version":"BIOMD0000000546","sim_time_s":108000},
 "export_signals":[{"signal_id":"miao2010.viral_load","value":3.29e5,
   "unit":"copies/mL","ci_95":[3.283e5,3.289e5],"transfer_lag_s":null}],
 "watchdog":{"status":"OK","divergence_score":0.0}}
\end{lstlisting}
The ABM adapter emits the same envelope with
\texttt{formalism}=\texttt{"ABM"}, \texttt{model\_version} pinned to
\texttt{covid-tissue-models@5b7e42c+OISA\_bridge}, a \texttt{grid} field
(\texttt{"90x90x2"}), and export signals
\texttt{sego2020.immune\_cell\_count} and \texttt{.recruitment\_cytokine}
(day-1 example: median 6 agents [ensemble range 3--11]).

Each adapter loads its published model verbatim and emits ISSL through the
standard model API (roadrunner for the ODE, an IPC bridge steppable for the
CC3D ABM); setup is in \S\ref{sec:valid-setup}.

\subsection{The Configuration Graph}\label{sec:configgraph}
The configuration graph is a YAML or JSON-LD file that fully specifies the
composition, the orchestrator's sole input at initialisation. It declares
six elements: \texttt{models[]} (id, formalism, executable,
\texttt{delta\_t\_s}), \texttt{edges[]} (source/target routing with
constant- or model-derived \texttt{lag}), \texttt{transfer\_models[]}
(lag-computing adapters), \texttt{global\_clock} (start/end/checkpoint
interval), \texttt{calibration} (EHR source and biomarker map), and
\texttt{wildcard\_namespace} (non-OBO entities such as novel cytokines or
patient-specific biomarkers; full schema in the repository). This
declarative separation of identity, wiring, and temporal parameters
implements the CURE extensibility requirement: any model node can be
substituted without modifying the graph structure.

\subsection{The Orchestrator Engine}\label{sec:orchestrator}
The orchestrator is a nine-component server process that reads the
configuration graph at startup, connects to all model processes, and
manages the run: (1)~ISSL ingestion (JSON-LD + SI units + Mahalanobis
OOD); (2)~temporal scheduler (priority queue by \texttt{next\_step\_due});
(3)~causal resolver (topological sort + one-tick back-edges);
(4)~constraint engine (mass conservation + parameter bounds); (5)~state
registry (versioned checkpoint snapshots + provenance); (6)~transfer
dispatcher (\texttt{SignalQueue}); (7)~calibration bridge (EHR ingest,
deferred); (8)~output aggregator (GIS $\to$ checkpoint log + UQ
propagation); (9)~watchdog (health poll, pause/resume/rollback). Components
1, 4, 5, 9 are implemented and covered by the 79-test suite; deferred
sub-capabilities (OOD detection, \texttt{ROLLBACK} recovery, PROV-O URIs,
auto-rollback) are interface-specified but not yet tested.

\textbf{Temporal scheduling.} At each GSimT tick the scheduler dispatches
step commands to models whose \texttt{next\_step\_due} equals the current
GSimT: the ODE (6\,h) steps every tick, the CC3D ABM (24\,h $=$ 72\,MCS)
every fourth.

\textbf{Causal resolution.} The resolver topologically sorts the DAG per
tick. At each 24\,h boundary the order is \texttt{ABM.emit()} $\to$
\texttt{ODE.accept()} ($n_{\text{immune}}\!\to\!T_{E_T}$) $\to$
\texttt{ODE.step()} $\to$ \texttt{ODE.emit()}, the ODE viral load queued for
the ABM's next \texttt{Accept}, the one-tick back-edge delay that breaks the
circular dependency while preserving biological causality.

\subsection{A Third Formalism: Stochastic Boolean Signalling}
\label{sec:boolean}

To test whether OISA's formalism-agnosticism is structural rather than an
artefact of the ODE+ABM pair, we added a categorically different formalism:
a continuous-time stochastic Boolean network. We use \texttt{TregModel} (35
nodes), a CD4$^+$/regulatory-T-cell network distributed verbatim in the
pyMaBoSS suite~\cite{pymaboss} (\texttt{test/TregModel\_InitPop.bnd}),
executed by MaBoSS~\cite{stoll2017maboss}, only to exercise a third
formalism and with no biological claim. Its adapter follows the same
non-invasive contract, modifying neither the \texttt{.bnd} logic nor the
\texttt{.cfg}, adding only \texttt{Emit()}/\texttt{Accept()}.

\paragraph{Coupling.} Antigenic drive from the ODE
(\texttt{miao2010.infected\_fraction}) maps to two continuous input rates
of the Boolean model, the TCR activation rate \texttt{\$ActTCR2} and the
IL-2 milieu \texttt{\$ExtIL2}, via the graded (non-thresholding) coupling
map. The Boolean model is relaxed to its quasi-steady-state attractor within
each 6\,h GSimT tick (intracellular signalling equilibrates in
minutes--hours $\ll$ tick; the FOXP3 attractor probability is stable over
$t=30$--$80$ a.u.). Its output, the FOXP3$^+$ attractor probability
\texttt{maboss\_treg.treg\_fraction}, feeds back onto effector immunity: the
effective effector count seen by the ODE is
$n_{\text{eff}} = n_{\text{immune}}\,(1-w\,p_{\text{Treg}})$, with
suppression weight $w=0.5$ (sensitivity quantified in
\S\ref{sec:valid-sens}), closing an ODE\,$\to$\,Boolean\,$\to$\,ODE loop
through the one-tick signal-lag mechanism. The ISSL \texttt{formalism}
enumeration, previously closed over \{ODE, ABM, PDE, \dots\}, was extended
additively with a \texttt{BOOLEAN} member; this backward-compatible change
is itself evidence that adopting a new formalism touches the \emph{contract}
rather than any existing model.

%%==============================================================
\section{Validation}\label{sec:validation}
%%==============================================================

The influenza A viral dynamics coupling (Miao 2010 ODE + Sego 2020 full CC3D
ABM) is the reference implementation because both models are independently
published, calibrated against experimental data, and their coupling tests a
biologically significant feedback loop: viral load drives immune
recruitment; CTLs modulate viral clearance.

\subsection{Validation Setup}\label{sec:valid-setup}

\textbf{Models.} The ODE component is
BIOMD0000000546~\cite{miao2010influenza}, a three-species influenza tissue
model (Ep: uninfected epithelial cells; Eps: infected; V: virus) with
kinetics calibrated against Murphy \emph{et al.} 1973 murine data, loaded
verbatim from BioModels by libroadrunner.

The ABM component is the complete spatial tissue ABM of Sego \emph{et al.}
2020~\cite{sego2020modular}, running in CompuCell3D 4.8.0 on a
$90{\times}90{\times}2$ voxel grid ($\approx$\,8{,}100 epithelial sites;
voxel length 4\,$\mu$m; six cell types incl.\ Immunecell,
\texttt{typeId}=5). All 12 published steppables (viral
replication/internalization/secretion, cytokine production/absorption,
immune killing/seeding/recruitment, chemotaxis, oxidation; full list in the
repository) are registered without modification. The simulation runs at
20\,min/MCS (72 MCS $=$ one 24\,h tick; 1{,}010 MCS $=$ 14 days), with three
diffusion fields (Virus, cytokine, oxidator) solved by
\texttt{DiffusionSolverFE}.

The single addition is \texttt{OISABridgeSteppable} (frequency 72\,MCS),
which runs inside CC3D but contains no biological equations: it counts
Immunecell agents, writes the count to \texttt{abm\_out.json}, raises an
\texttt{abm\_ready} sentinel, and blocks until it is deleted, while ODE
signals arrive non-blocking via \texttt{ode\_signal.json}. CC3D runs as an
adapter subprocess with no shared memory, isolating the ODE solver from
the CC3D engine.

\textbf{Adapter metrics.} Total modification to either published model is
\textbf{zero lines}; adapter code totals $\sim$300 lines: Miao 2010 wraps
libroadrunner ($\sim$60\,LOC), Sego 2020 couples subprocess lifecycle + IPC
($\sim$180\,LOC) with an in-process \texttt{OISABridgeSteppable}
($\sim$60\,LOC).

\textbf{Runtime UQ.} The ABM runs as a rolling ensemble of $N\!=\!50$
parallel CC3D instances (distinct MersenneTwister seeds, isolated IPC
directories) launched by \texttt{Sego2020Ensemble}; the orchestrator
blocks on the slowest at each 24\,h barrier, so per-tick wall-clock cost
is $\max(T_i)$ not $\sum T_i$ ($N\times$ memory remains). At each boundary
the ensemble adapter aggregates \texttt{n\_immune} into its median and
2.5/97.5\textsuperscript{th} percentiles, and the ODE adapter integrates
three times per tick (median, lower, upper input) to report viral-load
bounds. Uncertainty thus originates in the stochastic ABM and is carried
through the deterministic ODE at each checkpoint.

\textbf{Model-derived transfer lags.} ISSL signals carry an optional
\texttt{transfer\_lag\_s} field; when non-null the orchestrator's
\texttt{SignalQueue} defers injection until the delay elapses. A reference
\texttt{BloodTransitAdapter} (first-order blood-transit ODE, Donskoy \&
Goldschneider 1992; $\tau=4.0$\,days) demonstrates this, independently
validated (8 tests), though the \S\ref{sec:valid-bio} influenza run uses
constant-lag edges.

\textbf{Signals.} Two ISSL signals are routed:
\texttt{miao2010.viral\_load} (copies/mL) ODE~$\to$~ABM and
\texttt{sego2020.immune\_cell\_count} (CC3D Immunecell count) ABM~$\to$~ODE.
The viral load maps to the CC3D recruitment signal
(\texttt{ImmuneRecruitmentSteppable.totalCytokine}) rather than the Virus
diffusion field, as tissue viral spread is already handled by the Sego 2020
steppables at their native resolution.

\textbf{Coupling constant $\kappa = 3.5{\times}10^{-7}$\,AU$\cdot$mL/copies.}
$\kappa$ is fixed by one dimensional constraint, not tuned: the physiological
viral peak ($10^{6}$--$10^{7}$\,copies/mL, geometric centre
$3.2{\times}10^{6}$) must map onto the centre of Sego 2020's functional
recruitment range ($10^{-2}$--$10^{2}$\,pM, geometric centre $1$\,pM) so the
borrowed recruitment machinery operates in its designed regime. This yields
$\kappa = 1\,\text{pM}/(3.2{\times}10^{6}\,\text{copies\,mL}^{-1}) =
3.2{\times}10^{-7}$, matching the operating value to within $0.04$ orders of
magnitude; $\kappa$ is thus pinned by functional-range matching rather than
free. Varying it by $\pm 1$ OOM shifts immune onset by $\pm 1$--$2$ days
without altering the \texttt{n\_immune} trajectory shape
(\S\ref{sec:valid-sens}).

The \texttt{sego2020.immune\_cell\_count} signal sets
$T_{E_T} = n_{\text{immune}} \times 100$\,CTL/mL in the SBML, modulating the
CTL killing term $k_E \cdot \mathrm{Eps} \cdot T_{E_T}$ (Miao 2010,
Table~1). The 100\,CTL/mL-per-agent factor is consistent with published
peak CTL densities ($10^{3}$--$10^{6}$\,CTL/mL) for a tissue volume
$\approx 0.01$\,mL. Since $T_{E_T}$ is adaptive while the CC3D Immunecell
agents are innate effectors, this mapping is a model-level approximation
for demonstrating coupling, not a claim about CTL kinetics.

The GSimT tick is GCD(6\,h, 24\,h) $=$ 6\,h, giving 56 checkpoints over
14 days (ABM steps at 24\,h boundaries, ODE every tick).

\textbf{Test suite.} Validation comprises 79 automated pytest tests: 23
ODE, 20 ABM, 11 Boolean (MaBoSS), 8 blood-transit adapter, and 17
integration tests, all traceable to published figures or parameter tables;
UQ tests verify \texttt{ci\_95} fields are non-null given ensemble input and
that tighter input bounds yield narrower output bounds.

\subsubsection{Coupled-System Biological Face-Validity}
\label{sec:coupled-valid}

The checks above establish orchestration correctness and each model's
plausibility in isolation. A distinct question is whether the
\emph{coupled} system reproduces temporal features of real infection that
no single model was fit to. We test this by comparing emergent observables
($N\!=\!50$ ensemble, median [2.5th--97.5th percentile]) against independent
published murine influenza-A kinetics (Table~\ref{tab:plausibility}, lower
block).

\textbf{Anti-circularity.} The comparison avoids crediting features fit
during calibration. (i)~Miao 2010 was calibrated to murine
\emph{viral-load} data~\cite{miao2010influenza}, so the viral-peak day is a
temporal \emph{anchor}, not a validated prediction. (ii)~The Sego 2020 ABM
derives from a SARS-CoV-2 simulator~\cite{getz2020sarscov2} with
recruitment machinery not fit to influenza, so recruitment-onset timing is
independent of both calibrations. (iii)~The coupling ($\kappa$, CTL
scaling, back-edge lag) was set from first principles and sensitivity
analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:valid-sens}), not fit to any joint viral$+$immune
series, so the clearance day is a genuine coupled-system property. (iv)~We
compare \emph{timing} and \emph{normalised shape} only: \texttt{n\_immune}
is a grid agent count, not a physiological one, so absolute magnitudes are
never compared.

All three emergent features fall within or immediately adjacent to the
published ranges. The viral-clearance day (median 9.5\,d) sits just after
the day 8--9 window in which 60\%/40\% of mice clear
infection~\cite{myers2021influenza}, and the recruitment onset (day 1)
matches the innate-response
timescale~\cite{lv2014kinetics,iwasaki2010innate}. Because clearance
timing emerges from the ABM$\to$ODE CTL pathway and was not fit to any
joint dataset, this is \emph{face-validity}, weak but genuine support
that the composition behaves realistically in time, not
\emph{prediction}.

\subsubsection{Parameter Sensitivity (coupling parameters)}
\label{sec:valid-sens}
An approximate $3\!\times\!3$ sweep of the two coupling parameters
($\kappa$ and \texttt{\_N\_IMMUNE\_TO\_CTL\_PER\_ML}) over $\pm$1~OOM,
holding the ABM ensemble fixed via a qualitative onset-shift proxy rather
than re-simulating $N\!=\!50$ per cell, is reported in the companion data
(\texttt{results/sensitivity\_analysis.json}). The scaling factor dominates
(a $10\times$ increase accelerates day-14 clearance by 2--3~OOM, multiplying
$T_{E_T}$ in the killing term); $\kappa$ acts more weakly via recruitment
timing. Across all 9 combinations, peak
$V \in [7.84{\times}10^{6},\,9.09{\times}10^{6}]$ and $V_{14}\!<\!1\%$ of
peak, so the trajectory is robust within $\pm$1~OOM of the chosen values.
Model-internal parameters (already calibrated by the original authors) are
out of scope; full coupled global sensitivity (Sobol) is future work.

The third formalism introduces one further coupling parameter, the Treg
suppression weight $w$ in
$n_{\text{eff}}=n_{\text{immune}}(1-w\,p_{\text{Treg}})$
(\S\ref{sec:boolean}). Because $w$ is a coupling hypothesis rather than a
fitted constant, we swept its full admissible range
$w\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0\}$ with a complete 14-day tri-formalism run at
each value. Three findings support robustness. First, the viral peak is
\emph{invariant} to $w$ ($9.09\times10^{6}$ copies\,mL$^{-1}$ at day 2.25
for all five runs): the peak precedes effector recruitment, so the
Treg-mediated suppression term cannot affect it. Second, the effect on
clearance is monotone but small: residual viral load at day 14 rises from
$0.092\%$ of peak at $w{=}0$ to $0.103\%$ at $w{=}1$, a $\sim$12\% relative
change across the \emph{entire} range, so no plausible value overturns the
qualitative clearance outcome. Third, and most relevant to the UQ
contribution, the propagated viral-load \texttt{ci\_95} relative width is
monotone in $w$ (16.1\% at $w{=}0$ down to 7.2\% at $w{=}1$): stronger
suppression shrinks the effective-effector term and hence its uncertainty
contribution to the viral-load interval. The chosen $w{=}0.5$ sits mid-range
and is consistent with the $\sim$1:1 Treg:Teff ratio at which suppression
assays report $\approx$50\% inhibition.

\subsection{Heterogeneous Cross-Formalism Uncertainty}
\label{sec:xf-uq}

The three coupled formalisms define uncertainty in three structurally
different ways (Table~\ref{tab:uq-taxonomy}). The deterministic ODE has no
intrinsic uncertainty: its \texttt{ci\_95} is \emph{propagated} post-hoc by
mirror lo/hi roadrunner instances driven by its input bounds. The ABM's is
an \emph{ensemble} dispersion, the 2.5--97.5th percentile band across
$N\!=\!50$ stochastic replicates, available only after $N$ runs. The
Boolean model's is \emph{native}: MaBoSS estimates each attractor
probability $\hat p$ from \texttt{sample\_count} trajectories within a
single run, reported as the 95\% Wilson score interval on $(\hat p, N)$,
bounded in $[0,1]$ and maximal at $\hat p = 0.5$, the most ambiguous
cell-fate decision.

\paragraph{Fusion at the boundary.} At the ABM\,$\otimes$\,Boolean coupling
node, the effective-effector interval is composed by interval arithmetic
respecting each factor's monotonicity,
$n_{\text{eff}}^{\text{lo}} = n^{\text{lo}}(1-w\,p_{\text{Treg}}^{\text{hi}})$,
$n_{\text{eff}}^{\text{hi}} = n^{\text{hi}}(1-w\,p_{\text{Treg}}^{\text{lo}})$,
so a \emph{single} transported interval fuses an ensemble-derived bound
(ABM) with a native-sampling bound (Boolean), each provenance recorded in
the signal. Injected into the ODE bounds runners, this makes the resulting
viral-load \texttt{ci\_95} carry uncertainty from all three formalisms.

\paragraph{Results.} Over the 14-day, 56-checkpoint tri-formalism run
($\texttt{sample\_count}=20000$), all 168 emitted ISSL records validate
against the (Boolean-extended) schema with zero errors and no null
\texttt{ci\_95}. Nominal dynamics are preserved, viral peak
$9.09\times10^{6}$ copies\,mL$^{-1}$ at day 2.25, matching the
two-formalism baseline. Interval ordering
($\text{lo}\le\text{point}\le\text{hi}$) holds at every checkpoint for all
three formalisms (0/168 violations), the ODE adapter's min/max sort
correctly handling the sign reversal of the immunity\,$\to$\,virus map
(more effectors $\Rightarrow$ less virus). As an internal
propagation-consistency check, the relative half-width of the viral-load
\texttt{ci\_95} tracks the fused effective-effector interval monotonically
($r=0.93$), confirming no bound is dropped or double-counted en route; it
grows from 0\% (pre-recruitment) to $\approx$11.5\% during clearance
(day 13.75) as the ABM ensemble band and Boolean Wilson interval both become
non-degenerate (Fig.~\ref{fig:xf-uq}).

\section{Discussion}
%%==============================================================

\subsection{OISA and the CURE Guidelines}
We interpret OISA's design against the CURE
guidelines~\cite{sauro2025cure}, supplemented by FAIR alignment. OISA maps
cleanly to each criterion: \emph{credibility} via ISSL \texttt{ci\_95} +
ensemble bounds + the watchdog \texttt{divergence\_score};
\emph{provenance} via \texttt{envelope.model\_version}
(\texttt{BIOMD0000000546} / commit-SHA + bridge tag for the ABM);
\emph{understandability} via \texttt{envelope} + \texttt{continuous\_state}
+ \texttt{export\_signals} per checkpoint; \emph{reproducibility} via
JSON-LD schema and commit-SHA \texttt{model\_version}; \emph{extensibility}
via edge-wired config-graph substitutability; and \emph{automated guideline
checking} via constraint engine + watchdog each tick.
Notably, OISA implements credibility at the \emph{composition} level: a
mis-specified scaling factor silently miscalibrates CTL killing even when
constituents are individually valid, and the explicit
\texttt{\_N\_IMMUNE\_TO\_CTL\_PER\_ML = 100} makes this auditable at the
multi-model scale with zero internal modifications.

\subsection{Limitations}
No biological novelty is claimed: the coupling validates that OISA
orchestrates heterogeneous published models with zero modification to
either, not any new immunological result.
Validation covers a single organism (murine) and disease (influenza A),
for which in-vivo longitudinal tissue-level data are not published in
directly comparable form. The biological check
(Sec.~\ref{sec:coupled-valid}) is emergent-feature \emph{face-validity},
not \emph{prediction}: no parameter of the coupled configuration was fit to
or tested against simultaneous in-vivo viral$+$immune measurements, and
absolute magnitudes are not compared. Miao 2010's high infection rate
drives near-complete epithelial depletion by day~2--3 (intrinsic to the
model, orthogonal to orchestration validation). The
\texttt{\_N\_IMMUNE\_TO\_CTL\_PER\_ML\,$=\,$100} conversion and the
$\sim$3~OOM mismatch between $V_{\text{total}}\!\approx\!1$\,mL (ODE) and
the $\approx\!10^{-3}$\,mL tissue patch (ABM) are folded into the single
functional-range constraint that pins $\kappa$ (\S\ref{sec:valid-setup})
rather than modelled term-by-term; predictive deployment must instead add
explicit $V_{\text{tissue}}\!=\!V_{\text{systemic}}\times
(V_{\text{patch}}/V_{\text{total}})$ normalisation and recalibrate against
paired data. Two limitations are specific to the third
formalism. First, the Boolean model is relaxed to its quasi-steady-state
attractor each tick, justified when signalling equilibrates fast relative
to the 6\,h tick (stable for FOXP3 over $t=30$--$80$ a.u.) but requiring
sub-tick stepping for tick-timescale signalling. Second, uncertainty is
fused at boundaries by \emph{interval arithmetic} on bounds, not by
convolving distributions: conservative and provenance-preserving, but
yielding no joint density and able to over-cover when ABM-ensemble and
Boolean sampling errors are correlated (Monte-Carlo density propagation is
the natural extension). The Treg suppression weight $w$ is a coupling
hypothesis bounded by sensitivity analysis (\S\ref{sec:valid-sens}) rather
than fitted to Treg:Teff data.

%%==============================================================

\section{Conclusion}
%%==============================================================
OISA contributes runtime uncertainty propagated from a stochastic ABM
ensemble through a deterministic ODE, model-derived transfer lags on edges,
and a composition-level biological plausibility engine. Coupling two
independently published influenza models through adapter-only integration,
with zero changes to either, shows that formalism-agnostic composition with
runtime UQ is achievable and that emergent bidirectional-coupling behaviour
neither model produces alone is recovered. The same contract admits a third,
categorically distinct formalism, indicating the approach generalises beyond
the ODE--ABM pair.
